Marks that work at sixteen pixels and six metres

Brand identities, logos, icon systems, marketing creatives and 3D posters.

Graphic design is where I started: posters in college, then identity and campaign work for startups, products and global brands. Most of it still ends up moving, because I usually animate the logos I draw.

Introduction

A logo has to carry an idea in one shape. I look for the idea first, in the name, the language or the product, and only then start drawing.

Thanini's mark is built from Tamil letterforms because the platform teaches in Tamil. Log2Base2's logo is a piece of code. Plato's mark is the philosopher himself, drawn simply enough to work as an app icon.

Below: two full identity systems, a wall of logos, the marketing and social work that carried them, and a campaign built around a 3D gorilla.

Branding and identity

Complete systems: mark, wordmark, construction, applications and motion.

Plato

Identity for a SaaS product at MindBridges that helps enterprise teams manage projects and knowledge. The mark is a friendly line drawing of the philosopher: approachable enough for a product, clear enough for an app icon. I also wrote the brand guidelines and built the team's SharePoint brand hub.

Log2Base2

Creative campaign: Mr. Kong

One 3D character carried a Log2Base2 campaign across a video ad, seasonal posters and print. Its premise: even a gorilla can learn to code.

The founder asked for a 2D ad. I pitched the idea in 3D instead and built the character myself, then carried him into posters and seasonal greetings.

See how the ad was made

  1. Concept: a gorilla learning to code, so anyone can.
  2. Modelled, rigged and animated the character in Blender.
  3. Video ad with voice dubbing and sound.
  4. Posters composited from 3D renders and photography.
